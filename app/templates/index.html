How it works

Z3 is a theorem prover: give it variables and rules, and it either finds values that satisfy every rule or proves that none exist.

  1. Your question becomes a few constraints on one move: which piece, where it lands, what it must do.
  2. Those sit on a base model of this position: how pieces move, what attacks what, and that your king must be safe afterwards.
  3. SAT means a move exists, and the model is the move. UNSAT means those conditions cannot all hold, and Z3 can say which ones clash.
  4. Every answer opens in three steps: plain-English constraints, the solver verdict with its model, and the Z3 code for the question, with a link to the base model it runs on.

Scope: one move in the current position. Castling, en passant and move history are handled by python-chess, and every answer is cross-checked against it.
